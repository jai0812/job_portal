What the portal is #

SharmaJai Job Portal collects job postings from employers' own career sites and from community-kept job lists, checks them all day, and shows them in one fast list per country. It was built for students and early-career job seekers, and especially for international students in the United States, who need to know whether an employer sponsors visas before they spend an evening on an application.

Four things set it apart from a general job board:

It reads the source

Jobs come from the systems employers publish them on (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever and others), so they appear minutes after they're posted, not days later on a reposting site.

It answers the visa question

Every US employer carries its H-1B record from USCIS data, and a posting's own words about sponsorship always win.

It's honest about level

"Early career" means 0–2 years. Titles that say Junior but ask for five years are moved where they belong.

It remembers for you

Mark what you've applied to, what you're waiting on and what you've declined, and see it all in your own analytics.

Create an account

  1. Press Create free account on any page. Sign in and Create account are two tabs on the same page.
  2. Press Sign up with Google and pick your Google account. That's all: the account is made with your Google email and you're signed in.
  3. Or type your email and a password of 8 or more characters, and press Create free account.

Sign in, passwords and sessions

  • Sign in with Google, or with your email or username (made from your email, e.g. priya.k) and password.
  • Made your account with your email, then sign in with Google using that same address? It's the same account; Google is simply added as a way in.
  • Made your account with Google? You can add a password any time with the 🔑 button.
  • Change your password with the 🔑 button at the top right of the job page. That signs you out on other devices.
  • A sign-in lasts 12 hours. After five wrong passwords in 15 minutes, sign-in pauses for 15 minutes.
  • Forgot your password? The site owner can reset it for you; there's no email reset yet.

The tabs #

The bar at the top of the job page switches between job markets and tools. Each job tab downloads only its own list, so switching is fast after the first visit.

Tracking your applications #

Your marks are yours alone: other people never see them, and they follow you to any device you sign in on.

Marking a job

  • Click the box in the Applied column: Applied (with today's date). Click again to clear it.
  • Hold the box (or right-click): Applied, Wait (a checkpoint you'll come back to), Decline (you decided against it), or Clear.
  • The stage dropdown on the right sets any stage: saved, interviewing, offer, rejected, hidden.

Analytics

The Analytics tab reads only your own marks: total applications, the last 7 days against the 7 before, your daily average and streak, interview rate, how many checkpoints and declines you have, and what share went to H-1B sponsors. Below that: applications per day, by sector, by finance-firm kind and by pipeline stage.

Quick-copy bar

Applications ask for the same things again and again: your LinkedIn, GitHub, portfolio, a sentence about yourself. Scroll the job list and a bar slides up from the bottom: click any saved value to copy it. Notes & edit opens your links, saved answers and a notes box. They're saved to your account.

Startup radar #

A separate view of what's happening in startups, useful for applying and for keeping up. Every day the portal reads:

  • Y Combinator's company directory: new batches, team sizes, what each company does, founders and their LinkedIn pages.
  • SEC Form D filings: young US companies that just raised money, with the amount.
  • Funding news from TechCrunch, Crunchbase News, Sifted, EU-Startups, BetaKit and Inc42, and Launch HN posts.

Each startup gets a category (AI & ML, fintech, health & bio, developer tools…) and a stage: early (under $5M raised), second round (Series A–B, under $40M) or advanced (Series C and later). Founders and public company email addresses (like jobs@ or careers@) are shown when the startup publishes them. News is checked every 3 hours, everything else daily.

Where jobs come from #

Employers' career sites (500+)

Most employers publish jobs through a handful of hiring systems, and those systems serve the same list to everyone. The portal has an adapter for each one:

SystemUsed by, for example
WorkdayBanks, insurers, defense and many large employers (country filters let the portal ask for US, India and Canada separately)
Oracle RecruitingJPMorgan Chase, Texas Instruments, Citizens
Greenhouse · Lever · AshbyMost tech companies and startups
SmartRecruiters · Phenom · WorkableMid-size employers, staffing firms, Indian companies
EightfoldNetflix and others
Company APIsAmazon, Microsoft and TikTok publish their own job feeds

Big non-tech employers (retailers, insurers, hospitals) post thousands of jobs, so for them the portal keeps only software, data, IT and HR roles. In Canada it also asks each large employer for HR roles by name.

Job lists on GitHub (40+)

Students and companies keep public lists of new-grad roles and internships (Simplify, SpeedyApply, Zapply and many more, including India and Canada lists). These are often the first place a new posting shows up. The portal reads each list's table or data file, keeps the list's own flags (closed, US citizens only, no sponsorship) and links to the employer's posting.

Job Bank Canada

The Government of Canada's job board, searched for HR, payroll, software and data occupations. It's checked every hour, with a full pass through every search every 6 hours, keeping to the 5 seconds between requests the site asks for.

What isn't read

A few sites refuse automated requests (some defense contractors and a regional hiring system used in India). The portal respects that and doesn't try to get around it; those employers' jobs still appear when a job list carries them.

The H-1B column #

The column answers one question: is this employer likely to sponsor a work visa? It's decided in this order, and the first answer found wins:

  • The USCIS numbers are initial plus continuing H-1B approvals for fiscal year 2023 from the USCIS H-1B Employer Data Hub, the latest full year published. Employers file under legal names ("Amazon.com Services LLC"), so the portal matches each company to its legal names; for banks and well-known employers those names are listed by hand.
  • A record is not a promise. An employer that sponsored 2,000 people may still hire this particular role without sponsorship. Use the number to decide where to spend your time, then check the posting.
  • Cap-exempt employers (universities, nonprofit research institutes, some hospitals) aren't limited by the yearly H-1B lottery. They're marked in the Company column and have their own filter.

Fields, immediate start, duplicates #

Fields (Category)

Decided from the title: SWE (software, developer, back end, DevOps, SRE…), DS/ML (data science, machine learning, AI, analytics…), Finance (quant, trading, investment, banking, risk, accounting…), IT (help desk, service desk, desktop support, IT technician…), HR (recruiter, talent acquisition, payroll, people operations…), otherwise Other. A list's own category (for example a data-only list) is added too.

⚡ Immediate start

Marked when the title or description says the job starts right away: "immediate start", "start date: as soon as possible", "ASAP", "urgently hiring", "immediate joiner", or a notice period of 0–15 days. For Job Bank Canada the portal opens each new posting once to read its start date.

Contract and part-time

When the career site labels a job contract, part-time or temporary, or the posting gives hours per week, the Role column says so ("Contract · 30 h/wk · W2").

One job, many sources

The same posting often appears on an employer's site and on three GitHub lists. The portal identifies a job by its cleaned-up application link, so it's one row, with "+ N more lists" in the Source column. A source with more detail (a description, a salary) fills in what another left out.

Architecture #

The whole portal runs as one Python web application with a SQLite database, on a small always-on cloud server. Background threads fetch and classify jobs; short-lived helper processes build the job lists; the web app serves those prebuilt lists straight from memory.

Scheduler

Background threads inside the web app: the 10-minute fast lane, the 30-minute refresh, the Job Bank sweep, the startup radar, and a self-check that restarts the app if it ever stops answering.

Fetchers

Up to six career sites are read at once; each site's own pages are read one at a time with short pauses. One broken site never stops the others; it's logged and retried next time.

Classifier

Plain rules, no AI guessing: word lists and patterns for level, field, immediate start and job type, plus lookups for H-1B records, finance firms, countries and cities.

Database

One SQLite file in write-ahead-log mode, so the job list can be read while fetchers write. Writes go in short batches, one at a time. A backup is taken every night.

List builder

After each refresh, a helper process reads every recent job once, adds the derived fields and writes one compressed list per tab, plus slimmer copies for guests and the home page board.

Web app

FastAPI on Uvicorn. It serves pages, the prebuilt lists (already compressed, so there's nothing to compute), and your own marks and analytics from the database.

What happens when you open a tab #

Because the whole list arrives at once, filtering, sorting and searching happen in your browser with no waiting. The US list is a few megabytes compressed; it downloads once per visit and each tab after that is instant.

Data model #

Running on a small server #

The portal runs on a free cloud server with 1 GB of memory. Building a list of 150,000 jobs used to need about 500 MB inside the web app, and Python never hands memory back. Two design choices keep it small:

  • Helper processes. List builds and startup scans run in short-lived processes. If memory ever runs out, the server stops a helper first, never the website, and the next run redoes the work.
  • Streaming. The list builder reads one job at a time, drops its description as soon as it has been read, and writes it straight into each tab's compressed file. Peak memory went from about 500 MB to about 90 MB, with byte-for-byte the same output.
  • Prebuilt and precompressed. Lists are compressed once when they're built, so serving a tab costs almost nothing, however many people open it.

Security #

Passwords

Never stored. Only a salted scrypt hash, which is slow on purpose so guessing is expensive. Unknown accounts take as long to check as real ones.

Sign-in limits

Five wrong passwords in 15 minutes pauses sign-in for that network and that account for 15 minutes; a site-wide limit slows down guessing spread across many networks.

Sessions

A signed cookie that scripts can't read, sent only to this site, only over HTTPS, valid 12 hours. Changing your password signs you out everywhere.

Requests from other sites

Anything that changes data must come from the portal's own pages; requests from other websites are refused.

New accounts

At most 5 new accounts an hour from one network and 120 an hour overall, plus a hidden field that catches form-filling bots.

Sign in with Google

The standard OpenID Connect flow with a one-time state, a nonce and PKCE. Only Google accounts with a verified email are accepted, and the portal never sees your Google password.

Who can do what

Checked on the server, not just hidden on the page: guests get only the slim lists, members only their own marks, and admin tools answer only the admin.

The page itself

A strict content policy allows only the portal's own scripts, styles and fonts; the page can't be framed by other sites; HTTPS is enforced.

The server

No open web ports: traffic arrives through an encrypted Cloudflare tunnel. The app runs as its own limited user, restarts itself if it stops, and the database is backed up nightly.

Privacy #

What's stored when you create an account: your email, a username made from it, a hash of your password (if you set one), the ID of your Google account (if you sign in with Google; the portal asks Google only for your name and email, and keeps the email), when you signed up and last signed in, the statuses, notes and dates you set on jobs, and anything you save in the quick-copy bar.

What isn't: no tracking scripts, no ads, no analytics services, no third-party fonts or code on any page. Your email is used only to sign you in; the portal doesn't send email.

Guests: nothing is stored about you. Your browser may remember small preferences (like whether the quick-copy bar is pinned) on your own device.

To delete your account and everything attached to it, ask the site owner.

Questions #

Is it really free?

Yes. No card, no trial, no paid tier. Browsing needs no account at all.

Why can't I see the Apply button as a guest?

Apply links, the H-1B column and tracking are what an account is for. Creating one takes an email address and a password.

A job says "Has sponsored" but the posting says no sponsorship. Which is right?

The posting. The column always shows the posting's own words when it has them; "Has sponsored" means the employer has sponsored H-1B visas in general, not necessarily for this role.

Why does a job say "date not given"?

Some lists don't publish when a job was posted. The portal then uses the time it first found the job, which is usually within minutes of it appearing.

An apply link is closed. Why is the job still here?

Employers close postings without notice. Jobs leave the list 45 days after posting; mark it Declined or Hidden to remove it from your view now.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. Every page works on a phone; the job table scrolls sideways for the wider columns.

Why is "Early career" missing a job titled Junior?

If the posting asks for 3 or more years, it's Experienced, whatever the title says. See Experience levels.

I forgot my password.

Ask the site owner to reset it. There's no email reset yet.

Can you add a company or a job list?

Most employers can be added in minutes when their career site uses one of the supported systems. Ask the site owner.

Glossary #

H-1B
The main US work visa for professional jobs. The employer files ("sponsors") it for you.
H-1B cap · lottery
The yearly limit on new H-1B visas, decided by lottery each spring.
Cap-exempt
Employers not limited by the cap (universities, nonprofit research institutes, some hospitals); they can sponsor an H-1B at any time of year.
USCIS
US Citizenship and Immigration Services, which publishes the H-1B approvals per employer used in the H-1B column.
FY2023
US government fiscal year 2023 (October 2022 – September 2023), the latest full year of employer data.
OPT · STEM OPT
Work permission for F-1 students after graduation: 12 months, plus 24 more for STEM degrees with an E-Verify employer.
Early career
On this portal: roles asking for 0–2 years of experience.
Fast lane
The 10-minute check of the top companies and all job lists.
Job Bank
The Government of Canada's national job board.
NOC
Canada's National Occupational Classification; Job Bank searches use its codes (for example 12101, HR officers).
Form D
A filing US companies make with the SEC when they raise money privately; the startup radar reads these.
Workday · Greenhouse · Lever
Hiring systems employers use to publish jobs and take applications.
